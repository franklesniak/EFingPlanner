# Safety and Emergency (Japan)

**Last reviewed:** September 2026

Most of this page is for you. The last part, headed "For parents", is for the grown-ups.

## Earthquakes

Japan has earthquakes. It's also one of the best-prepared places in the world for them. Buildings and trains are built with earthquakes in mind, and people know the safety routines well. If you feel one, stay calm. Follow the grown-ups and the staff around you.

## If you ever get separated

Most of the time, you're with your family. If you can't find them, first do what today's rule says. A grown-up names it each morning, and it's usually to stay where you are.

Then ask a helper who is right there, and stay in your spot while you do. Call out or wave, even to a helper you can see farther away. The only time you move is to a meeting spot that morning's rule named.

Helpers you might have right beside you:

- **A station worker** in uniform.
- **A shop or security worker** with a name tag.
- **A police officer** at a *koban*, a small neighborhood police post.
- **A worker** at a *konbini*, a convenience store.

If more than one kind of helper is right there, ask the uniformed worker or the police first. Helping people is their job. A shop worker helps as a kindness.

## Two phrases for your card

You copy these two phrases onto your [If I Get Separated card](../../../framework/trip_starter/if_i_get_separated_card.md). A grown-up checks the wording.

| What it means | In Japanese | Say it like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Please help | Tasukete kudasai | tah-soo-keh-teh koo-dah-sigh |
| I'm lost. I got separated from my family. | Maigo desu. Kazoku to haguremashita. | my-goh dess. kah-zoh-koo toh hah-goo-reh-mahsh-tah |

The card itself tells you what else goes on it.

## Emergency numbers

Your card has room for two emergency numbers: one for the police, and one for fire and ambulance. The Japan National Tourism Organization lists both on its "Japan Visitor Hotline" page, under "Police" and "Fire/Ambulance". A grown-up checks each number there, or on another current official page, and writes the date checked. Then you write the checked numbers on your card.

Someone can make the call for you: a grown-up, a shop worker, or a police officer.

## For parents

The rest of the safety side is yours. Each item is on the [adult-only logistics checklist](../../../framework/parent_guide/adult_only_logistics.md):

- **Travel advisories**, checked on the U.S. Department of State's Japan travel page before you go.
- **Monitoring** for alerts during the trip, from the official sources named below.
- **Travel insurance**, decided before you go.
- **Medical coverage**, confirmed with each traveler's own insurer for care outside the United States.
- **Emergency contacts**, including the nearest US embassy or consulate.

**Local natural hazards.** Earthquakes are the main one, and a strong quake can bring a tsunami warning on the coast. The Japan Meteorological Agency issues earthquake, tsunami and weather warnings. The Japan Tourism Agency's Safety tips app sends those alerts in English. Apps change, so check that it still works before the trip. Heat and typhoons are on the [seasons, weather, and events page](seasons_weather_events.md).
