# Airports and Arrival Basics (Japan)

**Last reviewed:** September 2026

This page is about the first day of the trip. Getting there sets the shape of that day, more than anything you choose to do. Grown-ups book the trip from the airport into the city.

## Which airport, and why it matters

Tokyo has two international airports, Haneda and Narita. Haneda is much closer to central Tokyo. Narita is farther out, so the ride into the city takes a lot longer. At the end of a long flight, that extra leg is tiring. The difference is big enough to plan around.

Most international flights to the Osaka area land at Kansai International Airport. It sits on an island in the bay, southwest of the city. Osaka's closer airport, Itami, mostly has flights within Japan.

Other big cities have international airports too. Nagoya, Fukuoka and Sapporo are three of them.

Which airport a family lands at is part of choosing flights, so it's a grown-up's call. Routes and timetables change, and so does the time each ride into the city takes. To find it, check the airport's own website or a route planner close to travel.

## Getting from the airport into the city

There are four kinds of ways in:

- **Trains and subways** -- regular trains that link the airport to the city.
- **Airport express trains** -- faster trains with space for bags. Some sell reserved seats only.
- **Airport buses** -- often called limousine buses. They go to big stations and hotels, and your bags ride underneath.
- **Taxis** -- door to door, and the most expensive choice.

With lots of luggage, pick a ride that ends close to where you're staying. Fewer changes mean fewer stairs with heavy bags. Services and fares change, so a grown-up checks the current options on the airport's official website before booking.

## The arrival day is a day to plan

On arrival day, the main job is getting from the airport to where you're staying, and settling in. Plan it that way. On your day cards, day one is marked Easy, and often day two as well.

The time difference for your family is on your [Trip-Basics card](../../../framework/templates/trip_basics.md). A grown-up fills that card in.

## Luggage changes what the day can hold

Luggage forwarding and station coin lockers can free your hands on arrival day. The [transportation basics page](transportation_basics.md) explains both.

## What stays with the grown-ups

Grown-ups book the airport trip into the city. They also book any airport service that needs a reservation. That can be a reserved train seat, a flat-fare taxi or shared shuttle, or sending bags on from the airport.
