# Language Basics (Japan)

**Last reviewed:** September 2026

People in Japan speak Japanese. This page shows how the writing looks, and gives you a small set of words to try. A few words, said politely, go a long way.

## What the writing looks like

Japanese uses three kinds of writing, often mixed together in one sentence:

- **Hiragana** -- rounded letters. Each one stands for a sound.
- **Katakana** -- sharper letters that also stand for sounds. They're often used for words borrowed from other languages.
- **Kanji** -- characters that stand for whole words or ideas. They came from Chinese writing long ago.

Japanese can also be written in the letters English uses. That's called **romaji**.

Train stations and big signs often show English or romaji next to the Japanese. Airports and big sights do too. Menus and small signs often use Japanese only. The [food basics page](food_basics.md) explains how people order from those.

## Words to try

Each word has a sound guide. Say each part the way it looks, and give the parts about the same weight. At the end of words like *desu* and *masu*, the "u" is almost silent.

| To say | In Japanese | Say it like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hello | Konnichiwa | kohn-nee-chee-wah |
| Thank you | Arigatou gozaimasu | ah-ree-gah-toh goh-zye-mahss |
| Excuse me, or sorry | Sumimasen | soo-mee-mah-sen |
| Please (when asking for something) | Onegaishimasu | oh-neh-guy-shee-mahss |
| Yes | Hai | hi |
| No | Iie | ee-eh |
| Do you speak English? | Eigo o hanasemasu ka? | ay-goh oh hah-nah-seh-mahss kah |
| Where is the bathroom? | Toire wa doko desu ka? | toy-reh wah doh-koh dess kah |

*Sumimasen* is handy. It gets someone's attention, and it also says sorry.

## Words for a restaurant

These two cover ordering and paying:

| To say | In Japanese | Say it like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| This one, please (point to it on the menu) | Kore o kudasai | koh-reh oh koo-dah-sigh |
| The bill, please | Okaikei onegaishimasu | oh-kye-kay oh-neh-guy-shee-mahss |

Here are a few more. Pick the one you like best:

| To say | In Japanese | Say it like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Water, please | Omizu o kudasai | oh-mee-zoo oh koo-dah-sigh |
| It's delicious | Oishii desu | oh-ee-shee dess |
| Said before you eat | Itadakimasu | ee-tah-dah-kee-mahss |
| Said after you eat, to thank the cook | Gochisousama deshita | goh-chee-soh-sah-mah desh-tah |

## Landing on a page in Japanese

Sooner or later, you'll open a page that's all in Japanese. That's normal, and it means nothing went wrong. Lots of good pages are written for people who live in Japan.

What to do next is on the [trusted starting sources page](trusted_starting_sources.md), in its note on language. It covers the English toggle, translating a page, and when to ask a grown-up.

## Getting by

Visitors get by well. Staff at stations, hotels and big sights often speak some English. Pointing, a smile and a polite *sumimasen* do a lot of the work.
