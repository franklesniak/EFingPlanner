# Adult Logistics (Japan)

**Last reviewed:** September 2026

This page is for the adults, and every item on it is theirs. It covers rooms, lodging types, booking lead times, entry, luggage help, and where the safety details live. Check each item on official sources close to travel, and write down the date you checked.

## How many people fit in a room

Rooms in Japan are often smaller than a US family expects, and each room has a firm cap on how many people can sleep in it. Many hotel rooms are built for one or two guests. Rooms for three or four exist but are fewer, and connecting rooms are uncommon.

- **A larger or multi-generational party** often needs two or more rooms. That shapes the budget, and it shapes which lodging types work.
- **Children may count differently.** Many hotels let a young child share an adult's bed. Hotels differ on the age for that, and on whether that child counts toward the room's cap.
- **What to verify for each property:** the room's maximum occupancy, how it counts children, the bed sizes, and whether it can guarantee rooms next to each other.

Which lodging is priced per room and which per person is on the [money basics page](money_basics.md).

## Lodging types, from the adult side

- **Traditional inn (ryokan)** -- tatami-mat rooms, futon bedding laid out at night, and a *yukata* robe to wear. Baths are often shared, sometimes fed by a hot spring, and dinner and breakfast are often served at set times. All of this varies by inn, so check the one you book. How an inn prices its stay is on the [money basics page](money_basics.md). Shared-bath customs, and the age rules at baths split by gender, are on the [etiquette basics page](etiquette_basics.md).
- **Apartment-style or family hotel** -- multi-person rooms with a kitchen and a living space. A larger family can often stay together in one unit.
- **Licensed vacation rental** -- a house or apartment rented by the night. Rentals take guests under a licensing or registration system. Before booking, an adult checks that the property is currently licensed or registered. A listing commonly shows its license or registration number. The Japan Tourism Agency's minpaku (private lodging) portal explains the system.

## Entry authorization

What a US visitor needs to enter Japan is set by Japan's government, and it changes. An adult checks the current requirement on the official government source close to travel: the visa pages of Japan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs. The U.S. Department of State's Japan travel page is a useful cross-check. Write down the date you checked.

**Fake sites.** Fake and unofficial websites sell travel authorizations, or charge a fee to apply for one. Before paying anyone, an adult checks the requirement, and the official way to apply, on the government's own page. Reach that page from the government's main site, or type its address yourself.
