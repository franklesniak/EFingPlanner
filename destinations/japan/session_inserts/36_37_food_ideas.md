# Destination Notes: Food and Places to Eat (Japan)

**Last reviewed:** September 2026

These are foods to try in Japan, and the kinds of areas where people find them. Each one is an idea to research. You pick what goes on your list.

## Foods to try

- **Ramen** -- noodles in a hot broth, with toppings. Many regions have their own style.
- **Sushi** -- rice with fish or other toppings. Conveyor-belt sushi (*kaitenzushi*) is a casual way to try it.
- **Udon and soba** -- thick wheat noodles and thinner buckwheat noodles, served hot or cold.
- **Tempura** -- seafood and vegetables fried in a light, crispy batter.
- **Okonomiyaki** -- a savory pancake cooked on a hot griddle, often right at your table.
- **Convenience-store food** -- rice balls (*onigiri*), sandwiches and hot snacks. It makes an easy breakfast or a quick lunch.
- **Station food** -- lunch boxes (*ekiben*) to eat on the train, and quick meals inside big stations.
- **Regional specialties** -- each area has its own. Osaka is known for *takoyaki*, little balls of batter with octopus inside. Hiroshima has its own layered okonomiyaki, and Fukuoka has its own ramen. Look up the ones on your route.
- **Izakaya-style dinners** -- small plates to share at a casual pub-restaurant. A grown-up decides whether one fits your family.

## Where people find food

- **Big train stations** -- restaurant floors, food streets and lunch-box shops, all inside the station.
- **Department-store food halls** (*depachika*) -- counters of food to take away, usually in the basement. Restaurant floors are often near the top of the same building.
- **Food markets** -- such as Nishiki Market in Kyoto, Kuromon Market in Osaka and the Tsukiji Outer Market in Tokyo. Some markets ask you to eat your food at the stall where you bought it.
- **Street-food areas** -- such as Dotonbori in Osaka, where snack stands line busy streets.
- **Shopping streets** -- covered local streets with small shops, cafes and snack counters.

Markets and shops change what they sell, and when they open. Check anything you plan around.

## What a family may have to plan around

Food needs, such as allergies, can take planning. So can finding seats for a bigger group. Your pack's [food basics page](../reference/food_basics.md) says what to check for both. It also explains how ordering works, why everyday food is part of the trip, and where to find a local review site.
