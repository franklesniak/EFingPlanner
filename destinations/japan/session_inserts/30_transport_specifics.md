# Destination Notes: Trains, Transit, and Travel Cards (Japan)

**Last reviewed:** September 2026

These are the ways people get around in Japan. Use them to fill the "How we move" column of your notes table.

## Long-distance

- **Shinkansen** -- the fast "bullet trains" between big cities.
- **Limited express trains** -- fast trains to places off the Shinkansen lines, such as hot-spring towns and smaller cities.
- **Long-distance buses** -- slower than trains, and often cheaper.
- **Flights inside Japan** -- for the longest hops, such as to Hokkaido or Okinawa.

## Around a city

- **Local trains** -- JR (Japan Railways) lines and other rail companies' lines, in and around cities.
- **Subways** -- underground trains in the biggest cities.
- **City buses** -- handy for sights away from a station. Kyoto has many of those.
- **Ferries** -- boats to islands, such as Miyajima.

## On foot and by taxi

- **Walking** -- often the best way to see a neighborhood. Station transfers add walking and stairs too.
- **Taxis** -- good for short hops, heavy bags and late nights.

## Where the rest lives

Travel cards, rail passes, luggage help, and how much walking a day can hold are in your pack's [transportation basics page](../reference/transportation_basics.md). It also names route planners to try. Use one that still works, or a map's Directions tool, to check how long a ride takes. Times change, so check each ride when you plan it.
