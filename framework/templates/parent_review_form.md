## The good-enough standard

Good enough means an adult could act on it. It doesn't mean finished, polished, or right.

Some standards won't fit every checkpoint. Write "not needed here" beside those. When your dates are already booked, Checkpoint 1 is a confirmation, so read "recommend" as "explain": can they say what the chosen season means for the trip, and whether it fits?

## Questions to ask

- What source helped you most, and why?
- What would we lose if we chose this option?
- What would make this day tiring?
- What needs adult verification?
- If we had to shorten the trip, what would you cut first?
- Is this fact from an official source, a review, a guidebook, or AI?

## What to avoid

- Choosing the answer for them
- Rewriting their recommendation in adult language
- Turning every worksheet into a lecture
- Requiring perfection before moving on
- Taking over the fun research decisions
- Letting them handle bookings, payments, accounts, or private data

Praise the move, not the mind. "You checked a second source." "You stopped at the stop point." Not "you're so smart" — that one quietly teaches that being right is the point.
