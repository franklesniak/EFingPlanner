# Hotel Comparison Card

Use one card per place you might stay, so you can compare options fairly instead of going on the first one you saw. **You compare. The adults book.**

You don't need one of these for every hotel that exists. Do at least one card for each place you might sleep overnight. Do two only when that base is still genuinely undecided and there are enough real options to compare. Where one option is already obvious, one card is enough. Four or five cards for the whole trip is plenty, and that's a finished job. This can be split across several sittings.

The cost row is the price for one night: for **one room**, or for one person where the place charges that way. If your family needs two rooms, a per-room night costs about twice that. Write "per person" beside a per-person cost. Keep each card in the unit the place uses, so you never mix the two up, and let a grown-up do the multiplying when you compare a per-room place with a per-person one.

**About room counts.** Rooms in some places are smaller than the ones you're used to, and they often cap how many people can stay in one room. Connecting rooms and true four-person rooms can be uncommon. So a bigger family may need more rooms than you'd expect. Check each hotel's limit on people per room. That limit is one reason a grown-up decides the room count and does the booking.

If this card runs past two pages, shorten Pros, Cons and Review themes first. Keep the cost, the date you checked, the verification row and the decision status — those are what make the card honest.
