# Educators and Pretend Trips

How to run this project with a class or a group, or at home with a trip nobody will book.

## Who this page is for, and the honest trade

This page is for a teacher, a librarian, a homeschool co-op leader or an after-school leader who runs the project with a group. It is also for a parent who wants to run it at home with a trip nobody will book. Both run the same shape, a pretend trip. The page calls the adult who runs it "the teacher". At home, that is you.

The trade, in three parts:

- **What a pretend trip keeps:** the research, the source habits, the trade-offs, the Stop Points, and the student's own calls inside the pretend plan.
- **What it gives up:** the family decision that the student's work feeds, the ["here's how your plan shaped what we booked" reveal](coaching_and_support.md#scripts-for-the-hard-moments) after booking, and Session 54, which compares the plan with the trip.
- **What stands in for that payoff:** a realistic scenario, the same official sources and tools a family uses, and an audience. The audience is the teacher at Checkpoint 1 and at the handoff, and, past First Taste, the class at the final presentation.

The project's design treats the family trip as the payoff that motivates the work. It also adds smaller motivators along the way, because a distant payoff is too far off to carry months of work alone. On a pretend trip, the smaller motivators carry the work, so it is a supported shape with a known cost: expect motivation to need more help. The "things I can't wait to see" page, which Session 01 starts, is the project's main motivation tool, and the short First Taste path keeps the finish close.

## The one script, said once at Session 01

Before the student reads Session 01's Steps, say something close to this, once:

> "In this class the trip is pretend. The research is real, your sources are real, and your recommendation is real work. Nobody is booking anything."

At home, open it with "For this project".

Session 01 still tells the student that they are the planner for "a real family trip", and that the grown-ups have picked where. Those lines are written that way on purpose: for a family, they are what makes the work matter. Leave the pages as they are. The script, said once, tells the student how to read every page after it.

## Adult setup, in place of Session 00

Do these five steps once, before the first session.

1. **Turn on a filter.** Use the school's or library's content filter, or a kid-safe search filter at home. [Privacy and safety](../docs/privacy_and_safety.md) says which research still needs an adult nearby.
2. **Fill in one Trip-Basics card** for the pretend trip, on a copy of the [Trip-Basics card](../trip_starter/family/trip_basics.md):
   - The destination: a place that has a destination pack in this repository.
   - How the party travels.
   - If the party flies, a pretend home airport: the nearest large one, with its code.
   - The time difference to the destination, which you look up.
   - A pretend maximum trip length.
   - The travel party, written by role, and how many travelers that makes. For example: a parent, a grandparent, a younger cousin, and the planner.
3. **Fill in one assumptions page** for the pretend trip, on a copy of the [Current Family Travel Assumptions page](../trip_starter/family/current_family_travel_assumptions.md):
   - A pretend season window.
   - A pretend budget band, in the kid-sized form the student uses.
   - A rough trip shape, and the likely arrival and departure places.
   - The mobility, dietary, sensory and medical rows, written for the pretend travelers. "None known" is fine.
4. **Choose AI.** The default is no. In a school, choose yes only if the school's policy allows an AI tool. A yes follows the [AI use rules](../docs/ai_use_rules.md), from the tool's age check to the adult-operated pattern. Record the choice on the assumptions page.
5. **Give each student a copy** of the [trip starter kit](../trip_starter/README.md), with the two filled pages in it.

One filled card and one filled assumptions page serve the whole group, so every student plans the same pretend trip from the same pretend facts.

## Privacy in a group

A teacher and classmates see this work, so the home rules on [privacy and safety](../docs/privacy_and_safety.md) apply with more force. Read that page and the [AI use rules](../docs/ai_use_rules.md) once. This section adds only what a group changes.

- **Pretend travelers only.** Every profile, interview and poll answer is about a pretend traveler, and that includes the planner's own profile in Session 02. A student may add their own interests there.
- **A student's own details stay off the pages.** Leave every student's own relatives off the pages, along with any student's own health, sensory, stamina, food or access needs.
- **Names, addresses and photos.** Leave off the names of classmates, relatives and anyone else a student knows, along with home addresses and birthdates. Never post a photo of a filled page.
- **Source details.** The Source Log still records each source's title, author or organization, and web address, as Sessions 04 and 06 ask. A person who is a source is written by their job, as the [citation style](../docs/citation_style.md#person) says.
- **The "if I get separated" card.** Skip it. It holds where a family stays and a parent's phone number, and a group should collect neither. Sessions 49, 50 and 53 mention it.
- **School rules.** Follow the school's own rules for research online, video, student records and any AI tool.

## Who does what

The teacher is the grown-up on every page. Where a page asks a grown-up for a fact or a choice, the teacher gives one for the pretend family. Where a page says "your family", read it as the pretend travel party.

- **Checkpoint 1 (Session 14)** is the one checkpoint on First Taste. It is finished once the student has shared it with the teacher, who fills in the [parent review form](../templates/parent_review_form.md) for the pretend family.
- **Checkpoints 2 to 5** are teacher reviews with the same four decisions a family uses: Approved, Approved with changes, Needs more research, and Park this decision for later. [Review checkpoints](review_checkpoints.md) explains each one.
- **Checkpoint 6**, the family decision meeting in Session 52, becomes a presentation to the class or to the teacher.
- **Session 53** ends with the student handing the finished plan to the teacher.
- **The student's own calls** hold inside the pretend plan: the one special pick, the must-do list and the order of the day. [What I decide](../student_guide/what_i_decide.md) draws that line, and the teacher honors it at every review, as a family would.

## What changes, session by session

Two rules hold on every page:

1. Skip every line for a family whose dates are already booked. A pretend trip keeps its dates open.
2. Stop every booking step at the research, because nothing gets booked, bought or reserved.

## Pacing

First Taste has 13 numbered sessions of 20-30 minutes each, plus a 20-40 minute review at Checkpoint 1. Session 15's second city card can take a sitting of its own, and a group that chose AI adds Session 09. So plan on 13 to 15 sittings. At 2-4 sittings a week, that is roughly 3-8 weeks, and it ends at a complete mini-plan. For the Core Finish Line and the full program, [time and effort](time_and_effort.md) has the ranges for both paces.

## Cross-curricular ties

The project touches several school subjects, so a teacher or a homeschool parent can log it as schoolwork, whether the trip is pretend or booked. Geography: regions, maps and routes. Math: budget estimates, currency and unit conversions, and estimating time and cost. Reading and writing: research, note-taking, a recommendation and the final presentation. Executive-function and study skills: planning, prioritizing and self-monitoring. Cultural studies: etiquette, language basics and history. This page maps no standards, because standards vary by state and country.

## Before you rely on it

No child has piloted this curriculum yet, and no class has either. The [pilot flag in time and effort](time_and_effort.md#for-the-pilot-runner-is-first-taste-working) and its signals were written for a family, so treat every time estimate here as designed intent. If you run a pretend trip with a group, share how it went in the project's [Discussions](https://github.com/franklesniak/EFingPlanner/discussions). Discussions are public, so write about the group in general terms.
