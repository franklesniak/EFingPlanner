# How to Take Notes

A good note is short. The test is simple: read it again three weeks from now, and you still know what it meant and where it came from.

Every note you take in this project has the same four parts.

## The four parts of a note

1. **The fact.** What you found out, in one line.
2. **Why it matters for our trip.** One more line. If you can't think of a reason, you might not need the fact.
3. **The source.** Where it came from.
4. **A question for later.** The thing you wondered about while you were reading.

Part two is the one people skip, and it's the one that saves you. A page of facts with no reasons is a pile. A page of facts with reasons is a plan starting to form.

## Use your own words

Read the sentence. Look away from the page. Then write what it said.

That sounds like a small trick, and it does two jobs at once. It proves you understood the sentence, because you can't say it your own way if you didn't. It also keeps you clear of copying a book, which isn't note-taking and isn't your work.

If a wording is so good you want to keep it exactly, put quotation marks around it and write down the page number. That's a quote, and a quote is allowed when you say it's one.

## Where each part goes

Your four parts don't all live in the same place.

- The fact and why it matters go on your page or your card for that topic.
- The source goes in your Source Log.
- The question goes in your [question parking lot](../templates/question_parking_lot.md).

Splitting them up feels odd for about a week. Then you'll find you can answer "where did that come from?" without reading everything again.

## Short beats long

Fill in short blanks. Tick boxes. Write one line where one line will do.

You don't have to write paragraphs to be taking this seriously. Paragraphs are slower to write and harder to use later, and a tired planner stops writing them. Short fields you'll actually fill in are worth more than long ones you won't.

## Writing down the source

Write the source while the page is still open. Going back for it later takes three times as long, and sometimes you can't find it again at all.

Each kind of source has its own short form, and there are five of them: website, book, map, video, and AI. You don't have to remember what each one needs.

- [Simple citation forms](../templates/simple_citation.md) is the printable page. Copy it into your binder and fill in the form that matches.
- [Citation style](../docs/citation_style.md) is where the forms are explained, if you want to know why a field is there.

## If writing is the hard part

Writing is not the skill this session is testing. Getting the thinking down is.

So use whatever gets it down. You can say your answers to an adult who writes them for you. You can draw a note instead of writing it. You can use two words where a sentence feels like too much. All of those count as a finished note.
