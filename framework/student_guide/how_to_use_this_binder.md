# How to Use This Binder

This project runs for weeks or months, depending on the path your family picked, and it makes a lot of paper. Here's how to keep it in a shape you can still use at the end.

## One folder that grows

One folder. One notebook. That's the whole system.

Keep your finished pages in it, in rough order, newest at the back. Rough order is enough. You don't need to sort your finished pages, and you don't need tabs yet. Tabs come once, at the very end.

If you like strong structure and tabbing things as you go makes you happy, go ahead. That's a choice you're allowed, and it isn't the default. Most planners do better putting pages in one place and getting on with the work.

## The six sections you keep

Six things run the whole length of the project. They're six labeled sections inside your one notebook, so everything stays in one place.

- **Start Here.** Your [Trip-Basics card](../trip_starter/family/trip_basics.md), the [assumptions page](../trip_starter/family/current_family_travel_assumptions.md), your [trip goals](../trip_starter/family/family_trip_goals.md), and, if you did Session 02, your [traveler profiles](../trip_starter/family/traveler_profiles/README.md). Session 02 isn't one of the 13 First Taste sessions, so on First Taste this section is complete without profiles. This one is filled in first and barely changes after that.
- **[Source Log](../trip_starter/logs/source_log.md).** One copy of the table for every source you use. This is the one that makes your recommendation believable.
- **[Research cards](../trip_starter/research/README.md).** One card per place you look into, so two places can sit side by side.
- **[Decision log](../trip_starter/logs/decision_log.md).** What you decided, when, and why, with one Decision Record for each choice. It stops you arguing the same thing twice.
- **[Question parking lot](../trip_starter/logs/question_parking_lot.md).** Where a question goes when it would pull you off today's session.
- **[Cut list](../trip_starter/logs/cut_list.md).** The things you chose to leave out, kept on purpose.

Label a section, and every page of that kind goes there. That's it.

## Print as you go

Print or copy each session when you reach it. Don't print the whole project at once.

Printing everything up front looks organized and doesn't help. Pages get lost, sessions change, and a stack of forty blank pages is discouraging to look at.

Printing a whole phase at a time is a fine middle path if the printer is in someone else's house.

## Your one what's-next page

Whenever you can't remember what comes next, open your [progress tracker](progress_tracker.md). That page answers it.

## Back up your work in 30 seconds

Weeks of work in one paper folder is one spill away from gone.

If you work on paper, photograph or scan each finished page into a phone folder or a cloud folder. If you work in Google Docs, the cloud copy is already your backup.

Before you photograph a page, look at it once. Check there are no private details on it. Your "if I get separated" card stays on paper, in your pocket. The full rules are on one page: [privacy and safety](../docs/privacy_and_safety.md).

## At the very end

At the very end, put your finished pages in the order you did them and write what each one is on a sticky note. That's enough. Families who go on to the longer path get [Session 50, Final Binder Assembly](../sessions/phase_08_readiness_final/50_final_binder_assembly.md), which helps with tabs and order, and a [print index](../print_index.md) that says what goes in which tab. You don't need to think about tabs before then.
