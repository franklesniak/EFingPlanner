# Session 17: Deep-Dive City B

You are here: Phase 3 (Choose Places). Not a First Taste step. Previous: [16 Deep-Dive City A](16_deep_dive_city_a.md) | Next: [18 Deep-Dive City C](18_deep_dive_city_c.md)

**For parents:**

- Status: Core
- Planner skill: organizing information
- Estimated time: 20-30 minutes
- Parent involvement: none / independent work
- Materials: this session's Destination Notes, a device with the kid-safe filter on, your City Research Cards for City A and City B from Sessions 15 and 16, your season from Checkpoint 1, your [Source Log](../../templates/source_log.md)

## Goal

Take City B to full depth, and see how it stacks up against City A.

## Start Here

Lay your City B card next to your City A card. Circle one row on City B that's still blank. That's your start.

## Steps

City B is your other Session 15 card, the one that isn't City A. Usually that's the city you picked yourself. If City A got a new card in Session 16, both Session 15 cards are still open, so pick the one you're more curious about. The other can still become City C. Today you fill it in the rest of the way. You already know how the card works from City A, so this time the interesting question is what makes City B different.

Open this session's Destination Notes and look for City B. If it's on the list, read its one line. If you found City B on your own, that's fine too. Your own sources carry it. Either way, treat anything the Notes suggest as a lead worth checking.

Work down your City B card, with City A right beside it:

1. **Start with a comparing question.** What could you do in City B that City A doesn't offer? What does City A do better?
2. **Use a fresh source.** Find at least one source you didn't use for City A. A city's official tourism site is a strong place to start.
3. **Fill each empty row, then glance across.** Top sights, one memorable fact, food and culture notes, travel time, how many days, and season fit. When you finish a row, read City A's answer to the same row. Anything surprising?
4. **Compare the downsides.** Is City B more crowded than City A? Farther away? Harder with bags? Write at least one downside for City B.
5. **Add each source to your Source Log** as you go, with today's date.
6. **Fill the planning assumption rows** at the bottom of the card, the same way you did for City A.

Say out loud, or jot in the margin, one way City B is different from City A. It can help with your trade-off in Session 21.

## Workspace

Use your City B card, with your City A card beside it, and your Source Log.

You can say your answers to an adult who writes them, or draw them, if that's easier.

## Artifact Created

Your City Research Card for City B, now at full depth.

## Stop Point

You are done when City B's card has its top sights, one memorable fact, at least one downside, a season-fit note, and at least one dated source you didn't use for City A. You don't have to fill every line perfectly. "Ask an adult" or "not sure yet" are fine answers.

## Source Check

Log this session's Destination Notes in your Source Log, with their title and today's date, if you used them. Then log each other source you used. Official city or tourism sites are best for facts. Mark anything that can change, like prices or hours, so a grown-up re-checks it close to travel.

## Finish and Quality Check

Finished? Use the [Finish and Quality Check card](../../student_guide/finish_and_quality_check.md) in your student guide.

## If You Get Stuck

Stuck? Use the [When I'm Stuck card](../../student_guide/when_im_stuck.md) in your student guide.

## Optional Extension

If you have extra energy, pick the row where City A and City B are most alike. Find one detail that tells them apart, and add it to City B's card. If not, you are done.

## Parent Notes

City B is usually your child's own pick. Either way, let the comparison with City A be theirs. If they ask which city is better, turn it back to them: "What did you find?" The formal comparison comes in Session 21, so today a margin note is plenty. A child who used the same source for both cities has missed the point of step 2; ask where else they could look.
