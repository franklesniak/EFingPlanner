# Session 02: Family Traveler Profiles

You are here: Phase 0 (Setup). Not a First Taste step. Previous: [01 Project Kickoff](01_project_kickoff.md) | Next: [03 What Makes a Good Trip](03_what_makes_a_good_trip.md)

**For parents:**

- Status: Core
- Planner skill: organizing information
- Estimated time: 20-30 minutes
- Parent involvement: 5-minute check-in, plus adult relay help for unreachable travelers
- Materials: your filled-in [Trip-Basics card](../../templates/trip_basics.md) (for the roster), the [Traveler Profile template](../../templates/traveler_profile.md), the [Family Interview template](../../templates/family_interview.md), the grown-ups' [Current Family Travel Assumptions page](../../templates/current_family_travel_assumptions.md) to read, and a pencil

## Goal

Make one profile for each traveler on your trip, and interview at least one of them.

## Start Here

Start a Traveler Profile page for the first traveler on your Trip-Basics card. Write who they are to you in its first box. You've started.

## Steps

First, read the grown-ups' [Current Family Travel Assumptions page](../../templates/current_family_travel_assumptions.md) once. It says what the grown-ups already worked out -- the rough season, the rough trip shape, and any constraints they know about. A grown-up owns that page, so you read it, but you do not change it. Medical details stay with the grown-ups and off your pages.

Now start on the travelers. You're planning for a group, so you need to know what the group is like. A profile is a short page of facts about one person: what they like, what wears them out, and what they'd love to do.

Write each traveler **by relationship, not by name**. "A parent," "a grandparent," "a cousin," and "me" all work. That keeps private details off your pages.

1. Start one Traveler Profile page for each traveler on your roster.
2. Fill in these fourteen boxes on every profile:
   - **Traveler role** -- who they are to you, by relationship.
   - **Preferences** -- how they like to travel.
   - **Constraints** -- anything that limits what they can do.
   - **Interests** -- what they want to see or try.
   - **Food interests** -- what they like to eat, and anything they avoid.
   - **Preferred pace** -- busy days, slow days, or a mix.
   - **Stamina for long, busy days** -- high, medium, or take it easy.
   - **Comfort with lots of walking and stairs on a busy day** -- how much is fine.
   - **Any sensory sensitivities** -- crowds, noise, bright or flashing light, food textures. "None known" is a fine answer.
   - **Here for the whole trip, or only part** -- the adults decide the exact dates.
   - **One thing they might love** -- your best guess, or their own words.
   - **One thing that might make the trip tiring** -- the same, honestly.
   - **Any needs the planner should design around** -- step-free access, an elevator, an accessible room, help with luggage. You never ask why.
   - **Questions to ask** -- what you still want to find out from them.
3. **Interview at least one traveler yourself**, whenever there's someone you can talk to. Use the [Family Interview template](../../templates/family_interview.md) and ask the three questions on it: what they'd love on this trip, what they prefer, and what might tire them. Write the answers down in their own words where you can.
   - If someone is far away or hard to reach, ask them by text or call, or have a grown-up ask and bring back the answer. You can also interview whoever is around now and mark the rest "asked through a grown-up," or leave that answer open. Do not wait on anyone's schedule.
4. Read your profiles together and circle anything that clashes. One traveler wants busy days and another wants calm ones? Good. You found that out now instead of on day three.

Three of the boxes are **pacing factors**: stamina, comfort with walking and stairs, and sensory sensitivities. They decide how full a day can be. You'll use all three again later, when you check the pacing of your plan.

**Avoid private information.** Write what someone likes and what wears them out. Do not write medical details, birthdates, or anything private. Medical specifics stay with the grown-ups and never come into this project.

Asking three questions is also practice for something bigger. Later you'll bring your whole plan to the family and talk it through. Interviewing one person today makes that day easier.

## Workspace

Use one copy of the Traveler Profile template per traveler, and one copy of the Family Interview template for the interview you run.

You can say your answers to an adult who writes them, or draw them, if that's easier.

Your trip starter kit has a [traveler profiles folder](../../trip_starter/family/traveler_profiles/README.md). Finished profiles live there, filed the way that folder says.

## Artifact Created

Your traveler profiles, one per traveler, plus your family input notes from the interview.

## Stop Point

You are done when every traveler you could reach has a profile. At least one profile comes from an interview you did yourself, if there was anyone you could talk to. Mark the rest "asked through a grown-up" or "not decided yet." That's enough for today.

## Source Check

No new sources needed unless you looked something up. The interview answers are family input, so they go on your profile and interview pages.

## Finish and Quality Check

Finished? Use the [Finish and Quality Check card](../../student_guide/finish_and_quality_check.md) in your student guide.

## If You Get Stuck

Stuck? Use the [When I'm Stuck card](../../student_guide/when_im_stuck.md) in your student guide.

## Optional Extension

If you have extra energy, interview a second traveler, or add one question of your own to the interview page. If not, you are done.

## Parent Notes

Two things make this session work. The first is reach: if a traveler is not available, relay the question yourself and bring back the answer, so your child is never waiting on somebody's calendar. The second is the boundary on private information. Stamina, pace and sensory sensitivities are planning facts and belong on the page; the medical reasons behind them are yours and stay with you. If your child asks why a relative tires easily, answer it out loud rather than in the binder. The three pacing factors come back in the pacing review later, so a thin answer now is worth one more question today.
