# Session 07: Library Research Plan

You are here: Phase 1 (Research Skills). Not a First Taste step. Previous: [06 Book Research With a Guidebook](06_book_research_guidebook.md) | Next: [08 Web Research Practice](08_web_research_practice.md)

**For parents:**

- Status: Recommended -- a good one to do, and a fine one to skip. Do it if a library visit or an online catalog is easy for you right now.
- Planner skill: getting started
- Estimated time: 20-30 minutes
- Parent involvement: parent setup needed
- Materials: the library's online catalog or a library visit, a pencil, a blank [Library Book List](../../templates/library_book_list.md), your [Source Log](../../templates/source_log.md)

## Goal

Build a short list of books worth borrowing, or a plan for one library trip.

## Start Here

Write "Books to look for" at the top of a fresh page. You've started.

## Steps

Libraries lend travel books for nothing. That's the whole idea behind this session. A guidebook you borrow works exactly as well as one you buy, and you can borrow four at once and keep the one you like.

There are two ways to do this session, and either one counts.

- **If you build a list**, search your library's online catalog from home and write down what it has.
- **If you go**, write down what to look for first, so the visit is short and you come home with something.

You don't have to pick now. Start the list either way, and the list works for both.

**Five kinds of book to look for:**

1. A travel guidebook.
2. Children's nonfiction about your destination.
3. A food or culture book.
4. A history or geography book.
5. A picture-heavy overview book.

You're not trying to find all five. Two or three kinds is a good list.

Now build it:

1. Search the catalog, or ask a librarian, using your destination's name plus one of the five kinds above. "Travel guide" and "for kids" are useful words to add.
2. Write down each book you find: its title, its author, and whether the library has it right now.
3. If you're going in person, add the shelf number the catalog gives you. That number is how you find the book on the shelf in about a minute.
4. If a book is checked out, put a hold on it with a grown-up, or write "ask again later" and move on.

A librarian is a source too, and a good one. Asking "where are the travel books for kids?" is a completely normal question, and they hear it every day.

Keep this small. A list, or a plan. You're not signing up to read four books.

## Workspace

Use your [Library Book List](../../templates/library_book_list.md) page. Two columns is plenty: the book, and whether the library has it.

You can say your answers to an adult who writes them, or draw them, if that's easier.

## Artifact Created

Your library visit plan, or your book list.

## Stop Point

You are done when your list names at least one book from two or three of the five kinds above. A date for a library visit plus a short list to take with you counts too. Either one finishes this session.

## Source Check

A catalog search is a look-up, so record it. Put the library catalog in your Source Log with today's date. If a librarian helped you find a book or a section, record that too. The source type is a person. Write "a librarian" and which library it was, like "our city library" or "the school library." You do not need their name, and you do not need the branch. When you actually borrow a book, that book gets its own entry, with its title, its author or publisher, and the pages you used.

## If You Get Stuck

Stuck? Use the [When I'm Stuck card](../../student_guide/when_im_stuck.md) in your student guide.

## Optional Extension

If you have extra energy, look up one more kind of book from the list of five, or ask a librarian what they would pick for a kid planning a trip. If not, you are done.

## Parent Notes

This one needs you first: a library card, a catalog login, or a ride. That is why it is Recommended rather than Core, and skipping it costs your child nothing that Session 06 does not already teach. If you do it, let your child run the catalog search and do the asking at the desk. Placing a hold and talking to a librarian are both small, safe, real transactions with a stranger, and they are worth more here than the books are.
