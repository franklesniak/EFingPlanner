# Session 28: Map the Route

You are here: Phase 5 (Route, Length, and Transportation). Not a First Taste step. Previous: [27 Checkpoint 3 Top Experiences](../phase_04_attractions_experiences/27_checkpoint_3_top_experiences.md) | Next: [29 How Long to Stay](29_how_long_should_we_stay.md)

**For parents:**

- Status: Core
- Planner skill: planning realistic time
- Estimated time: 20-30 minutes
- Parent involvement: 5-minute check-in
- Materials: a device with a map app or map website (kid-safe filter on), your city shortlist from Checkpoint 2 (and the card for any whole region on it), your family's [current travel assumptions](../../templates/current_family_travel_assumptions.md) page (for the arrival and departure places), a pencil, your [Source Log](../../templates/source_log.md)

## Goal

See how your places sit next to each other, measured by how long it takes to travel between them.

## Start Here

Open a map app or map website. Type one city from your shortlist into the search bar, and find its dot. That's your start.

Here's how to read the map for trip planning:

1. Each city you're considering is a **dot**. Type its name in the search bar to find it.
2. Dots that look **close together** are usually closer cities.
3. What matters for planning is **travel time**: how many minutes or hours the ride takes. Click **Directions** between two dots, and pick the **train** to see how long the trip takes. If your family will get around another way, pick that one.
4. Two cities can look close but take hours by train. Two others can look far apart and be a fast train ride. **Trust the time the Directions show you.** The dots can fool your eyes.

## Steps

Maps are tricky at first, and "how near is near?" is a hard question for everyone. The travel time answers it.

1. **Find every place on your shortlist**, one dot at a time. If a place is a whole region, use the dot for the town you wrote on its card in Session 21. No town there yet? Choose one now with a grown-up, where you'd sleep or spend the day.
2. **Check the travel time for each hop your trip might make.** That means each overnight place to the next, and each base to each day trip. It also means where you arrive to your first overnight place, and your last one to where you leave. Skip one of those if the two places are the same, or if a grown-up hasn't written that place down yet. Include pairs that look far apart. On a trip with one base and no day trips, also time the trip from your base to your must-dos. Use **Directions**, and write each time in the Workspace table. Add rows if you need more.
3. **Answer five questions**, using travel time every time:
   - Which places are close together, a short ride apart?
   - Which places are far apart?
   - Which work as **day trips**, close enough to visit and come back the same day?
   - Which need **overnight stays**?
   - Would this route make us **crisscross** too much, with lots of long back-and-forth rides?
4. **Sketch your route.** Draw your cities as dots on a line from left to right, in the order you'd visit them. Start where your family arrives, and end where you leave, if the grown-ups have written those places down. Write the travel time on each line between dots.

Each city is still a movable block, as [Session 15](../phase_03_choose_places/15_city_research_cards.md) explained, so your sketch can change later by moving one block.

## Artifact Created

Your Route map notes: travel times between your places, which ones are day trips and which need an overnight, and a left-to-right sketch of the route.

## Stop Point

You are done when you have travel times between your places, and you've marked which are day trips and which need an overnight. A rough sketch is plenty. "Not sure yet" is fine for any pair you couldn't check.

## Source Check

Your map is a source, so give it a Source Log entry using the Map form on your [simple citation forms](../../templates/simple_citation.md) page: map tool, place or route searched, and date I checked it. Train times can change with the season or the day of the week, so mark them for a grown-up to check again before booking.

## Optional Extension

If you have extra energy, find the pair of cities that fooled your eyes the most: close on the map but slow to travel, or far apart but quick. Circle it on your sketch. If not, you are done.

## Parent Notes

Reading a map and judging distance is hard at this age, and that is normal. The four moves in Start Here are a scaffold, and your child can drop them once they can do this alone. If maps are hard even with the scaffold, the [differentiation guide](../../parent_guide/differentiation.md) applies: read the map together, use Directions for the train times, or sketch a simple left-to-right line of the cities in trip order. The crisscross question is the one that matters most for the next few sessions, because every long back-and-forth ride takes time away from the places themselves.
