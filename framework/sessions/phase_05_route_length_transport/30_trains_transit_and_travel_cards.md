## Goal

Learn how you'll get around, and what the grown-ups still have to check.

## Start Here

## Steps

Every place has its own ways of getting around. Your Destination Notes name the ones where you're going. This session teaches what each kind is for, so you can plan around them.

**Ways to get around:**

- **Fast long-distance trains** link cities that are far apart.
- **Local trains and subways** move you around inside a city.
- **Travel cards.** Many places sell a card you load with money and tap each time you ride. Whether visitors can get one can change, and so can the price. Grown-ups check what's available before the trip.
- **Walking** is often the best way to see a neighborhood, and the steps add up fast.
- **Taxis** help for short hops, late nights, and heavy bags.

**Moving your bags:**

- **Sending bags ahead.** In some places, you can send your bags to your next hotel. Then you travel light on a moving day. Grown-ups arrange it and pay for it.
- **Station lockers.** Many stations have lockers. You can leave your bags there for the day and explore without dragging them around.
- **Very large suitcases.** On some fast trains, a very large suitcase needs a special seat booked ahead, one with room for the bag behind or beside it. Grown-ups check the size rule on the official train website, because the rule and its numbers can change. That's one more reason sending bags ahead is a favorite family move.

**Tickets and planning:**

- **Rail passes.** A pass might save money, or it might cost more than single tickets. It depends on your exact route. The only way to know is to compare the two, and a grown-up checks before anyone buys one.
- **A big group.** Look at the number of travelers on your Trip-Basics card. A big group should plan to reserve seats together on long train rides, because open seats side by side can be hard to find. A small group can skip this.
- **A transit planner** is a website or app that plans a ride on trains and buses. Your Destination Notes may name one. Check that it's still working and up to date before you trust it.
- **Grown-ups buy the tickets and passes.** Your job is to work out what the family needs.

Now fill in the notes table in the Workspace, one row for each way of getting around you might use.

**Guess, then check.** Pick a trip your route map notes don't have yet, such as from one of your must-dos to another in the same city. In a new Source Log entry, write your guess for how long it takes by train or bus in the **What I learned** box: "My guess: ___." If your family won't ride trains or buses there, guess the time for the way you'll go. Then check it in a current transit planner, or in your map's Directions. Add what you found and the date. How close were you? Being off is normal, and your [planner mindset card](../../student_guide/planner_mindset.md) says why that's fine.

## Parent Notes

The place-specific names, such as the train lines, the travel card, and any bag service, are all in the destination pack. The session teaches only what each kind of transport is for.

Everything your child lists in the last column is yours to verify. Rail-pass value depends on the exact route, so compare it against single tickets before buying. Travel-card availability for visitors can change as well as the price. The large-suitcase seat rule, where one exists, is on the official rail site, and its size limit can change. Check that any transit planner is current before relying on it. Sending bags ahead and paying for it are adult tasks.

The travel-time guess is the second guess-then-check loop, after the ticket-price guess in Session 23. Being off is part of the practice.
