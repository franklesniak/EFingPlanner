# Session 25: Review Reviews Carefully

You are here: Phase 4 (Attractions and Experiences). Not a First Taste step. Previous: [24 Culture, History, Nature, Food, and Fun Balance](24_culture_history_nature_food_fun_balance.md) | Next: [26 Rank Attractions](26_rank_attractions.md)

**For parents:**

- Status: Core
- Planner skill: checking sources
- Estimated time: 20-30 minutes (one sitting); the second sitting can be its own
- Parent involvement: co-working recommended
- Materials: a device with the kid-safe filter on, a grown-up nearby for any video, a timer, your attraction cards, your [Source Log](../../templates/source_log.md), the [simple citation forms](../../templates/simple_citation.md) page for the Video form

## Goal

Learn to judge a review, a blog, or a video before you trust it.

## Start Here

Set a timer for 20 minutes. Then pick one attraction card, and write that place's name in the first row of the sitting one table in the Workspace. That's your start.

## Steps

Reviews, travel blogs and videos can help you choose what to do. They can also steer you wrong. This session comes in two sittings, and you need both. You can do sitting two on another day.

### Sitting one: four moves

1. **Ask the big question.** Whenever you read a review or blog, or watch a video, ask: "Who made this, and what are they selling?" They might be selling a hotel, a tour, a product, or just more views.
2. **Read sideways.** Open a new tab and look up the person or the website. Who are they? What do other sources say about them? This is the lateral reading move from Session 05, used on reviews.
3. **Look for what many reviews say.** One review is one person's day. Read several, and look for the themes that keep coming up. The star score alone tells you very little.
4. **Remember who the trip is for.** Famous and best for our family can be two different things.

Now practice. Find one review, blog post or video about the place you picked, with a grown-up beside you for any video. Fill in the sitting one table in the Workspace.

### Sitting two: why reviews can steer you wrong

Here are seven reasons a review can mislead you:

- **Review inflation.** Lots of places have high ratings, so a high score often means less than it seems.
- **Fake reviews.** Some are written by the business itself, or by people paid to write them.
- **Sponsored posts.** The writer got paid, or got the visit for free.
- **Influencers.** People with lots of followers are often paid to show a place.
- **Affiliate links.** Some links pay the writer when you buy something through them.
- **Ad-heavy blogs.** A page crowded with ads may be written to get clicks.
- **One-star and five-star reviews.** The angriest and happiest reviews are often the least typical. The middle ones usually tell you more.

Now practice again. Find one more review, blog post or video, and fill in the sitting two table.

### When the source is a video

A travel video is usually made to entertain you first. It can also be a paid ad, even with no "#ad" label on it. Labels are often missing, hard to find, or hidden deep in the description. The description often holds affiliate links, too. Many video makers earn money for every view, so exciting claims pay off for them.

Use the same moves: ask who made it, read sideways, and remember who the trip is for. If a video tells you a fact you want to use, check it on an official website before you write it down. Watch videos with a grown-up and the kid-safe filter on, and keep your timer running. Stopping when it rings is the same skill as stopping at a Stop Point.

A few video habits help too:

- Use a kids' or supervised setting if your family has one.
- Turn off autoplay.
- Skip the comments.
- Leave the suggested videos on the side alone.
- Keep your timer on and a grown-up nearby.

These habits add to your trust questions from Session 05. Keep using both.

## Stop Point

You are done for today when you've judged at least one review or video on the worksheet, written who made it and what they might be selling, and named one thing you'll check somewhere else. Sitting two can be another day. The session is finished when sitting two's rows are filled in as well. "Not sure" is a fine answer in any row.

## Source Check

Log each source you took a fact from. For a video, use the Video form on your [simple citation forms](../../templates/simple_citation.md) page: channel name, video title, date I watched it, what it helped with, the fact I checked somewhere else, autoplay off, and timer set. Then log the official site you checked that fact on. A review you only judged, and took no fact from, stays on your worksheet and needs no Source Log entry.

## Parent Notes

Stay nearby for this session, and alongside for any video. A kid-safe filter reduces exposure without removing it, and video brings autoplay, recommendation drift, comments and paid links; the [privacy and safety page](../../docs/privacy_and_safety.md) has the full rule. The habits in the video section are general ones. Your family's own settings on any given site are yours to set up.

Both sittings are required. Splitting them keeps each one short, and the second one carries the reasons reviews mislead, which is what makes the first sitting's moves stick.

If your child trusts a glowing review, ask the big question with them: "Who made this, and what are they selling?" Then read sideways together. That shared check is the lesson, and it works whether or not you know the answer.
