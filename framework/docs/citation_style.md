## Why we write down where a fact came from

A fact is worth what its source is worth. Writing the source down is what lets a young planner hand a recommendation to an adult and have the adult trust it. It is also what lets anyone check the fact again later, which matters because prices, hours and rules move.

There is a second reason, and it is the one children feel. A note written in the child's own words, with the source beside it, is the child's own work. Nobody has to wonder whether a page was copied.

A source line is short. It is a habit rather than a chore, and the forms below are deliberately small enough to finish in a minute.

## When a citation is required

Write a citation whenever an outside source was used. That means a book, a website, a map, a video, a review site, or an AI tool.

A review site is a website, so it uses the Website form. There is no separate form for reviews.

A session with no research step is never asked for a citation. If nothing was looked up, there is nothing to record, and an empty Source Log for that session is correct.

## The five forms

Five kinds of source, five forms. Use these field names exactly, on every page that reproduces a form, so a child meets the same words each time.

### Website

Website title, organization or author, page title, web address, date I checked it.

### Book

Book title, author or publisher, page number, date I used it.

### Map

Map tool, place or route searched, date I checked it.

### Video

Channel name, video title, date I watched it, what it helped with, the fact I checked somewhere else, autoplay off, timer set.

### AI

AI tool name, prompt I asked, date used, what it helped with, facts I checked somewhere else.

## The three verification fields

Three more fields sit under whichever form was used. They record the checking rather than the finding.

- What other source can check this?
- Verification source
- Date checked

The date matters more than it looks. A fact that was true on the day it was checked can be wrong by the time the family books, and a dated note says which one you are holding.

## The note structure

Every note the child writes has four parts, in this order:

1. The fact.
2. Why it matters for our trip.
3. The source.
4. A question for later.

The fourth part is the one people drop, and it is the one that keeps research moving. A question written down goes to the question parking lot instead of derailing the session it occurred to you in.
