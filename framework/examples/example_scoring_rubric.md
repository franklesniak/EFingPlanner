# Example: Scoring Rubric

This is a filled copy of the [Scoring Rubric](../templates/scoring_rubric.md). The pretend planner used the Lighter Rubric (3-criteria) to compare two cities, Florence and Venice.

## Lighter Rubric (3-criteria)

| Criteria | Option A | Option B | Option C |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What is it? | Florence: the city with the giant dome | Venice: the city on the water, with canals and boats | |
| Excitement (1-5) | 4 | 5 | |
| Family fit (1-5) | 5 | 3 | |
| Easy to get to and around (1-5) | 4 | 2 | |
| **Total** | **13** | **10** | |

The planner compared two cities, so Option C stays blank. The fuller version works the same way, with more rows to score.

After scoring, the planner wrote down the trade-off:

| Prompt | Your answer |
| --- | --- |
| My pick | Florence |
| What I gain by choosing it | An easier trip. The train from Rome is shorter, and we can walk everywhere. I also get the dome I starred. |
| What I give up by choosing it | The canals and a boat ride. My gut still says Venice, so I'll talk about it with a grown-up. |

## What to notice

- A high score for "easy to get to and around" means easier. Venice got a 2 because it's a long ride from Rome, and it has lots of bridges with steps.
- Family fit comes from what the family said they wanted. They like slow walks and good food, so Florence scored higher there.
- The total picked Florence, and the planner's gut said Venice. That's worth talking about, so the planner wrote it down.
- The scores helped the planner think. The planner still made the choice.
