# Example: How a Planner Thought About It

This page is the pretend planner thinking out loud about one choice from their [Trade-Off Report](example_tradeoff_report.md). They're talking it through with a grown-up, after the report is done.

## My question

We already had Rome and Florence. My scores had picked Florence over Venice, but my gut kept saying Venice, because of the canals and boats. So my question was: should our route add Venice too?

## What I found

On the map picture, Venice looked close to Florence. So I tried the map's Directions. The fast train from Florence takes about two hours. That's just the ride, though. We'd also pack up, get to the station, and find the new hotel. My day rules say a moving day is a light day. So adding Venice would use about half a day just to get there.

## What surprised me

Venice has no cars. People walk or ride a water bus. The guidebook says there are lots of bridges with steps. I pictured us carrying our suitcases up and down all those steps. I hadn't thought about suitcases at all until then.

## The trade-off

Route 1 gives us more time in each city and only one hotel move. Route 2 gives me the canals, but we'd pack up twice and rush Florence. The dome is the sight I starred first, and I want a fresh morning for it. So I picked Route 1. That means I give up Venice for this trip.

## What I checked, and who with

I checked the train times two ways. First I used the map's Directions. Then a grown-up sat with me while I looked at the train company's official website. The two times were close. I wrote them on my report with the date, April 2. I also asked a grown-up if one more hotel would still fit our budget band. They said they'd look into it.

## What I left for a grown-up

Train tickets, prices, and which hotel we stay in. Also whether the budget band still fits. Those are grown-up calls, so I wrote them in the last box of my report.

## Why I stopped there

I could have read about Venice all day. But two sources agreed on the train time, and more reading wouldn't change my answer. So it was good enough. A grown-up can still change the route, and my work still counts. My Venice card says "save for future", so it's ready for another trip.

## What to notice

- The planner said out loud what they gave up.
- They checked one fact in two places, with a grown-up, and wrote down the date.
- They left the money and the hotel to the grown-ups.
- They stopped when more looking wouldn't change the answer. That's [good enough is good enough](../student_guide/planner_mindset.md).
