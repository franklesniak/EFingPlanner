# Worked Examples

This folder holds pages that a pretend planner filled in for a pretend trip. Look at them when you're stuck and want to see what a finished page can look like. Your pages will look different, and that's fine.

## The examples

One planner made all six pages, for one trip. They're listed in the order the planner made them.

- The [Example Source Log](example_source_log.md) shows three short entries on a [Source Log](../templates/source_log.md). It helps when you wonder how much to write about one source.
- The [Example City Research Card](example_city_card.md) shows a [City Research Card](../templates/city_research_card.md) filled in for one city. It helps when you start a city card and wonder how long an answer should be.
- The [Example Scoring Rubric](example_scoring_rubric.md) shows the Lighter Rubric (3-criteria) from the [Scoring Rubric](../templates/scoring_rubric.md), used on two cities. It helps the first time you score your choices.
- The [Example Trade-Off Report](example_tradeoff_report.md) shows a [Trade-Off Report](../templates/tradeoff_report.md) that compares two routes. It helps when you have to name what a choice gives up.
- [How a Planner Thought About It](example_how_a_planner_thought_about_it.md) is the planner thinking out loud about one choice. It uses no template. It helps when you want to hear how a choice gets made.
- The [Example Daily Plan Card](example_day_card.md) shows one block card from the [Daily Plan Card](../templates/daily_plan_card.md). It helps when you build your day cards.

## For parents

These pages use Italy, a place that exists, so the reasoning in them is the kind your child will do: reading a travel time from a map, checking a season against the crowds, and naming what a choice gives up. Italy is also off target on purpose. The destination pack in this repository covers a different place, so for a family using that pack, no example answers a question their child is working on.

No session uses these pages, and your child can skip all of them. They are here for a child who stalls on a blank worksheet and wants to see a filled one.

The examples carry no prices, opening hours or ticket rules. Where a filled card would hold a figure, it holds a word such as "higher", a rough span of time, or a note to check an official source, with the date the pretend planner checked it.

If your own trip is to Italy, treat these pages as someone else's pretend research. Your child still checks every fact in their own sources and makes their own calls.
